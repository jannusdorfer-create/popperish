Hunt the zeros

Picture a landscape with a gold line across it. A zero is a place where the landscape touches the floor. Every zero anyone has found sits on the gold line, and the Riemann hypothesis says they all do. Here you hunt them yourself, with the same method as our engine, live in your browser.

Status: a proof was posted in November 2026. It is a claimed proof, checked line by line by the Lean program, and there is no human-readable version yet. The Clay Institute has not declared the problem solved. Nothing on this page proves anything.

How zeros keep their distance

If zeros were scattered at random, tiny gaps would be the most common. They are not. The bars are the gaps between neighbouring zeros, and they follow the curve for the energy levels of heavy atoms.
